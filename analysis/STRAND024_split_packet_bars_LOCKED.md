# FND-STRAND-024 — the split packet: bars locked first

Date: 2026-08-04. Commission: FND-STRAND-023's corrected redesign. The
question, now correctly idealized: one quantum modeled as a LOCALIZED
packet at real-bench intensity, coherently SPLIT into two half-packets
delivered to separated regions of one thermal chain — do the two regions'
clicks show sub-independence exclusivity (one click suppresses the
other), plain independence (a classical split behaving classically), or
bunching? ALL THREE branches are registrable at full volume, and the
INDEPENDENT branch is stated now to be as important as the EXCLUSIVE one:
it would register a measured LIMIT of the classical detector story — the
place where genuine quantum exclusivity is NOT reproduced — which the
corpus would keep at the same volume as any promotion.

## The main ensemble

## B1 — the exclusivity statistic

rho := p_double / (p_A_hat x p_B_hat), where p_A_hat, p_B_hat are the
SPLIT ensemble's own marginal per-region fire rates (self-normalized;
the single-lobe control cross-checks the marginals and is reported).
- EXCLUSIVE: rho <= 0.40 — one region's click suppresses the other
  below independence: the shared-origin quantum enforces one-click.
- INDEPENDENT: 0.70 <= rho <= 1.40 — the classical split behaves
  classically; NO exclusivity; registered as the measured LIMIT of the
  classical detector story (the point where quantum exclusivity is not
  mechanical in this engine), at full volume.
- BUNCHED: rho >= 1.80 — correlated double-clicking; registered as
  found (shared bath / collective mechanisms become the named suspects).
- INTERMEDIATE: as measured. Pricing note, honest: with S = 96 and
  marginals ~0.35, rho carries a standard error ~0.35; the three named
  branches are separated by >= 2 SE from each other's edges — adequate
  for the branch call, not the decimal, and the verdict language is
  committed accordingly.

## B2 — symmetry sanity (reported, no bar)
