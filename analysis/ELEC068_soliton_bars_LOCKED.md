# ELEC-068 — Does the coupled system admit a stable localized solution? Locked bars

## Commission
ELEC-067 showed matter couples to the fast channel through a cubic vertex that
strengthens in the inextensible limit, and that the back-reaction is attractive.
It named the next question: does the coupled system admit a stable finite-energy
localized solution, and what fixes its scale?

## Method, fixed before computing
Derrick scaling. Eliminate the longitudinal constraint exactly to get an
effective energy in the transverse field alone, then rescale psi(x) -> psi(x/L)
and examine E(L). A stationary point with E'' > 0 is a stable solution; E'' < 0
is a barrier, and collapse or dispersal is the fate.

## Locked bars
B1 THE ELIMINATION: substitute u' = -((k-T0)/(2k)) psi'^2 into the energy and
   report the effective density. State the SIGN of the quartic term.
B2 THE STATIC VERDICT, and it may be fatal: with only gradient and quartic
   terms, E(L) = A/L - B/L^3. Report the stationary point and its stability.
   IF E'' < 0 there is NO static soliton and the static electron collapses --
   which must be reported as a negative regardless of what follows.
B3 THE DYNAMICAL TEST, the point of the session: internal circulation or
   time-dependence contributes an inertial term ~ integral omega^2 psi^2 dx,
   which scales as +C L. Re-run the analysis with E(L) = A/L - B/L^3 + C L and
   report whether a stable branch exists, under what condition, and where.
B4 THE SCALE: if a stable branch exists, identify what sets L*, and state
   explicitly whether it is proportional to the strand spacing a. THIS IS THE
   DECIDING QUESTION for ELEC-066's conjecture: ELEC-057's no-go swept r_e ~ a,
   so a scale NOT proportional to a is outside that sweep, while a scale
   proportional to a is inside it and the conjecture dies.
B5 HONESTY: Derrick scaling establishes EXISTENCE OF A STATIONARY POINT under
   an assumed profile family, not a solution. It does not construct the
   solution, prove uniqueness, fix the amplitude, or connect to any measured
   electron property. State all four omissions.
