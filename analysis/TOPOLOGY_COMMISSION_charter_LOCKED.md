## THE TWO MODELS (equal footing, locked)

  DIRECT-ROPE: every atom has a dedicated rope connection to every
      other atom.
  LOCAL-WEAVE: each atom couples only to its local neighborhood;
      disturbances travel through the connected weave.

Both are rendered into equations using the SAME rope tension, linear
density, source strength, and boundary conditions. NEITHER side may
introduce corrective parameters after seeing results. In particular
(locked in advance): no rescue of the form "divide every rope's
strength by the total number of atoms in the universe" is permitted
unless that normalization FOLLOWS INDEPENDENTLY from the rope
mechanics, derived and registered before the comparison runs.

## THE DECISIVE EXPERIMENT (the spectator test; FIRST BRICK)

Hold two nearby atoms A and B in exactly the same configuration.
Add increasing numbers of distant, neutral SPECTATOR atoms:
M = 0, 8, 64, 512, ... Measure whether the following LOCAL
properties change:
  - inertial mass of A
  - energy stored near A
  - force between A and B
  - local wave speed
  - effective charge / coupling strength

In a genuinely local weave, sufficiently distant spectators must not
alter these local measurements except through an identifiable field
arriving from them. In an all-to-all model, every added atom creates
new direct ropes to A; the direct model then OWES an explanation for
why those ropes do not change A's energy, inertia, or interactions
-- and the no-rescue rule above binds that explanation.

FIRST-BRICK SCHEDULING (author's call, locked): the spectator test
is attempted ANALYTICALLY before any large simulation -- the energy
and force bookkeeping of both models under added spectators is
closed-form accessible, and an analytic verdict, if reachable,
outranks a numerical one.

## PRE-REGISTERED TESTS (all locked; a model must face every row)

  T1 ENERGY SCALING: energy per atom remains finite as the system
     grows. (Local networks scale ~N; complete networks carry
     ~N^2/2 connections.)
  T2 SPECTATOR INDEPENDENCE: distant neutral matter does not
     reprice local physics. (Do local constants depend on the
     universe's population?)
  T3 GAUSS LAW: flux through a sphere depends only on the enclosed
     source. (Do exterior endpoints improperly affect the field?)
  T4 STATIC FORCE: V(r) ~ 1/r, hence F(r) ~ 1/r^2. (A local 3D
     wave operator produces this naturally.)
  T5 PROPAGATION: no physical response before r/c. (Medium
     propagation vs instantaneous coordination.)
  T6 FLUX SPREADING: amplitude ~ 1/r with conserved total spherical
     flux. (Genuine three-dimensional transmission.)
  T7 ISOTROPY: direction-independence at large scales. (Does the
     discrete weave have a valid continuum limit?)
  T8 POLARIZATION: two transverse modes with controlled
     longitudinal contamination. (Can the structure carry
     electromagnetic waves?)
  T9 CLUSTER SEPARABILITY: two distant laboratories behave
     independently when interaction is negligible.

## REGISTERED VERDICT FORMS (exactly four; anything else is NO CALL)
