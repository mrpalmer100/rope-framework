# FND-017 — What sets T0, and why does the tensioned ground state not relax? Locked bars

## Commission
ELEC-080 closed the electron line and named the residue: a ground state under
tension is a ground state storing energy, and nothing in the corpus explains
what sets T0 or why the medium does not relax. That is a foundations question
older than the electron arc.

## Two questions, to be kept apart
Q1 WHAT SETS T0? Currently taken from hadronic measurement and carried
   everywhere as an input.
Q2 WHY DOES IT NOT RELAX? A ground state carrying tension appears to store
   energy that a system should be able to lower.

## What must be checked first, before any new argument
ELEC-053 proved the invariance theorem T0/Sigma = a^2/3 for any tube radius.
If that holds, T0 is NOT an independent quantity: T0 = Sigma a^2/3, so Q1
reduces to what sets Sigma and a. Both are already registered -- Sigma as the
corpus's ONE open number with two candidates and a named experiment
(QGATE-007), a as the Lorentz bound. Check this before treating Q1 as new.

## Locked bars
B1 Test the reduction numerically: does a = sqrt(3 T0/Sigma) reproduce the
   Lorentz bound for BOTH registered scale sets? If it does, Q1 is not a new
   open question and must be reported as already-registered.
B2 Q2 ON ITS OWN TERMS: is T0 stored energy, or a constraint force? For an
   INEXTENSIBLE medium, tension is the Lagrange multiplier enforcing the
   constraint, and a multiplier stores no energy -- it does work only when the
   configuration changes. Determine which reading the corpus's own postulates
   force.
B3 IF T0 IS A MULTIPLIER: what determines its VALUE? A multiplier is fixed by
   the state and the boundary conditions, not by a local property. Say what
   that implies.
B4 THE RESIDUE after both: state what remains genuinely underived.
B5 HONESTY: this is a structural argument from registered claims, not a
   derivation of T0.
