# QB-034 — the severing discriminator: Reading A killed, Reading B empty, and Mode 1 forced to be accounting rather than dynamics

Date: 2026-08-04. Commission: the operator asked whether the shared-ribbon
ontology could improve memory-based quantum repeater networks. The registry's
answer required a session rather than an opinion.
Benchmark: `benchmarks/quantum/qb034_severing_discriminator.py`.

## Bars (locked before computing)

1. The two readings of "severing" named FIRST, before any curve:
   **Reading A** (persistent severed ribbon) — decoherence converts intact
   pairs into Mode-1 pairs, which still correlate at E = −(a·b)/3.
   **Reading B** (ribbon destroyed) — severed pairs are uncorrelated, E = 0.
2. **Degeneracy test, mandatory and run before any confrontation:** a reading
   whose decay family is algebraically identical to the standard
   Werner/depolarizing family makes NO new claim and registers EMPTY. No fit
   quality rescues a degenerate model.
3. Only a feature standard dephasing CANNOT produce counts as a
   discriminator. Pre-committed candidate: a non-zero asymptotic floor.
4. **Confrontation scope, stated honestly:** the session may confront a
   qualitative, uncontroversial experimental fact without claiming any
   specific published number. A quantitative literature confrontation with
   citations is NOT performed here and must not be pretended.
5. A kill of Reading A is permitted and expected. QB-015's ontology is not on
   trial — only this decoherence extension.

## The computation

With p the intact fraction:

- Reading A: V = p + (1−p)/3 = **(1 + 2p)/3**
- Reading B: V = **p**

**Degeneracy test.** Reading B is algebraically identical to the standard
depolarizing family — registered EMPTY, unconfirmable and unrefutable by
decay data. Reading A is *also* a one-parameter Werner family, so at any
fixed time a severed fraction is indistinguishable from a depolarized
fraction. The difference is not in the form but in the **reachable range**.

**The discriminator.** As p → 0, Reading A gives V → 1/3, CHSH → 0.9428.
That is below the Bell threshold (V = 0.707), so it is not a Bell violation —
but it is a *persistent classical correlation that never decays away*.
Standard dephasing and Reading B both give V → 0.

## Verdicts

**Reading A is KILLED.** Stored entanglement in quantum memories is routinely
observed to decay to classical, uncorrelated outcomes. A residual V = 1/3
correlation surviving indefinitely in every entangled pair ever stored would
be among the most conspicuous anomalies in experimental physics; it is not
observed. Severing cannot be a persistent state of the ribbon.

**Reading B is EMPTY** by construction, per the degeneracy bar.

## What survives — the session's real product

1. **QB-015's ontology is untouched.** It was never on trial. The wall value
   1/3 remains the signature of severed *bookkeeping* — a description-level
   error about treating one object as two.
2. **Mode 1 is forced to be an ACCOUNTING mode, not a DYNAMICAL one.** The
   corpus had never had to say which, because nothing depended on the
   distinction until someone asked the ribbon to decohere. That disambiguation
   is the durable output: 1/3 is what you get when you *describe* the singlet
   wrongly, not a physical state a decohering pair passes through or lands in.
3. **Consequence:** decoherence must be modeled as the ribbon being cut
   (Reading B), and Reading B is empty — so the ribbon picture makes no
   distinguishable prediction about memory decoherence.

## The answer to the commissioning question

**No engineering lever for repeater networks.** The corpus now has a
benchmark establishing this rather than an opinion. This is the expected and
correct result: QB-015 reproduces the quantum correlations exactly (Tsirelson,
no-signaling identical), and a framework that lands exactly on standard
quantum mechanics cannot yield a better key rate. Had it claimed one, that
would have been a defect.
