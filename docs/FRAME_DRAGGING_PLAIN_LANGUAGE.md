## What frame dragging is

General relativity predicts that a spinning mass does not just attract things --
it slowly wrings the space around it. A satellite orbiting a spinning planet has
its orbit dragged around in the direction of the spin, very slightly, as if the
floor it stands on were slowly turning. This is the Lense-Thirring effect.
Gravity Probe B and the LAGEOS/LARES laser-ranging satellites have measured it
around Earth, and it matches general relativity's prediction.

## What causes it in the rope model

In this framework, everything is strands under tension, and a body's angular
momentum is not an abstract number -- by the registered rotation grant (GRV-104,
beta_J = 1), a spinning mass literally holds a *twist* in the weave it is
anchored to.

That twist cannot stay at the source. It conditions the surrounding mesh the same
way a mass-knot's stress conditions the mesh to produce ordinary gravity. Because
a neutral spinning body has no net "angular charge," a derived theorem (GRV-020's
no-monopole lemma) forces the influence to spread as a *dipole*: strongest around
the equator, falling as the inverse square, with a handedness set by the spin
direction.

The result is that the weave near a spinning body carries a faint standing swirl,
co-rotating with the body. Anything moving through that region rides a medium
that is being gently wrung. Its orbit precesses not because a force pushes it
sideways but because the medium itself is slowly turning. Where general
relativity says "spacetime is dragged" as geometry, the rope model says the weave
is *actually, mechanically* twisted. The picture is completely literal.

A useful image from the framework's own electromagnetic sector: a conduction rope
is a drill bit -- push it lengthwise and the winding converts the push into
rotation. A spinning planet is the drill bit run in reverse at planetary scale:
rotation pumps twist into the weave, and the weave's answering swirl is frame
dragging.

The framework's own numbers killed it (GRV-123 through GRV-125). The stiffness

So the two effects **decouple**: whatever causes the observed cosmic
birefringence, in this framework it is not the weave's handedness.
