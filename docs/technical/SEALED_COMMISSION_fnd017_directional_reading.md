## THE COMMISSION

The corpus registers, via ELEC-053's invariance theorem and FND-017's reduction,
the relation

    T0 = Sigma * a^2 / 3

where `T0` is the strand tension, `Sigma` the vacuum stiffness (energy density),
and `a` the mesh cell scale. FND-017 further establishes that `T0` is a Lagrange
multiplier conjugate to the inextensibility constraint, not a stored elastic
energy, and that no local derivation of `T0` can exist.

**The question, to be answered from the geometry alone:**

When a strand or inclusion displaces the medium through a mesh cell, does the
displacement couple to the **full cell cross-section `a^2`**, or to **one
direction's share, `a^2/3`**?

Equivalently: is the factor of 3 in `T0 = Sigma a^2 / 3` a statement that the
cell's energy is partitioned across three independent strand directions — such
that a single displacing object engages only one such share — or is it a
consequence of the invariance theorem's tube-radius integration that carries no
per-direction partition at all, leaving the full face `a^2` as the object a
displacement sees?

**What the answering session must produce:**

1. A verdict: `a^2` or `a^2/3`, or an explicit finding that the geometry as
   registered does not determine it (which is itself a permitted and useful
   answer, and must be stated rather than resolved by preference).
2. The provenance of the 3: trace it to where it enters. ELEC-053's derivation
   is the place to look. Whether it arises from direction-counting or from the
   radial integration is the crux, and the corpus already owns the material to
   settle it.
3. Whether the answer is a *consequence* of the registered relation or a
   *reading* of it. If it is a reading, say so; a reading is not a derivation.
4. A benchmark, if the verdict admits one.
