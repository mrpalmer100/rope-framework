## What R left, precisely
R's tether construction gives the Z_2 CLASS (pi_1(SO(3)) = Z_2, verified):
it makes the double-valued (spinor) representation ADMISSIBLE and uniquely
pairs it with fermi statistics. What the classical tether does NOT do is
SELECT the double-valued rep over the single-valued (scalar) one. R located
this precisely as "the occupancy of the tether class" and forecast it as
one of two remaining quantum bricks. S is the commission that tests whether
that brick can be laid.

## The ONE decisive distinction (the bar the whole commission turns on)
There are two very different things S could find, and they must NOT be
conflated -- conflating them is the only way this commission produces a
false result:

  ADMISSIBILITY (no advance): occupation makes the spinor rep NATURAL,
  available, or consistent. The tether ALREADY gave this. Re-deriving it
  is not laying the brick; it is restating R.

  FORCING (the brick laid): the occupancy quantum J0 acting on the Z_2
  tether class MECHANICALLY SELECTS the double-valued rep over the
  single-valued one -- the singlet is excluded, not merely less natural.
  This would fold the spin brick INTO the occupancy input R already
  carries, reducing R's two bricks to one.

S's entire value is distinguishing these. A session that shows
admissibility and reports it as forcing has failed the commission.

## THE HONEST FORECAST (stated on the charter so the session is not
## chasing a summit)
The likely outcome is ADMISSIBILITY, not forcing: R already showed the
classical tether did not force the rep, and adding J0 probably makes the

generically carries quantum content that occupation alone does not fix.
If so, S's result is "the two bricks are ONE quantum package (occupancy-
plus-selection), not two" -- a real SHARPENING of the wall, not a crossing
of it. S does NOT determine hbar and does NOT derive a mass under any
outcome; the absolute anchor (the alpha wall) stands behind it untouched.
The session runs for the sharpest boundary map, not for a summit.

## PHASE 1: DOES J0 FORCE THE REP? (targets-blind, admissibility-vs-forcing)

### The question
Take R's frozen tether structure (the rotating winding terminus, its Z_2
config-space class) and O's frozen occupancy quantum J0 (I_mode = m J0 on
the adiabatic invariant). Compute whether J0's action on the Z_2 tether
class MECHANICALLY EXCLUDES the single-valued representation, forcing the
double-valued (spinor) one -- or whether it leaves both admissible.

### Bars
- S1 (THE DECISIVE BAR -- forcing, not admissibility): the deliverable is a
  YES/NO on EXCLUSION of the singlet. "The spinor rep is natural/available/
  consistent under J0" is ADMISSIBILITY and is registered as NO-BRICK. Only
  a mechanical EXCLUSION of the single-valued rep counts as forcing. The
  session must state, in one sentence, which of the two it found, and must
  exhibit the exclusion (if claimed) as a mechanical fact, not a
  naturalness argument.
- S2 (FROM REGISTERED STRUCTURE ONLY): the computation uses R's frozen
  tether topology and O's frozen J0, no new structure introduced. If
  forcing requires an input beyond {tether Z_2, J0}, that input is NAMED
  and the brick is NOT laid by S alone (it would be a third input, which
  would WIDEN the wall, not narrow it -- a registrable finding).
- S3 (TARGETS BLIND): Phase 1 does not load the electron mass, the (4,7)
  quartic, or any spin-measurement value. Whether the forced rep (if any)
  matches the spinor requirement is Phase 2. The forcing question is
  answered on the {tether, J0} structure alone, before any target.
- S4 (NO SUMMIT REACH): the session may NOT attempt to determine hbar or
  the absolute scale from S -- that is the other brick (the alpha wall,
  Q-proven unreachable) and is out of scope. Any drift toward "and this
  also fixes the scale" is the contamination S4 forbids; S narrows the
  quantum boundary, it does not cross it.

## PHASE 2 (SEALED): DOES THE FORCED REP MATCH THE ELECTRON?

## Registrable outcomes (all acceptable)
1. FORCING (the brick laid, low prior): J0 mechanically excludes the
   singlet; R's two bricks reduce to ONE (the absolute anchor / alpha
   wall). The electron's spin is then FULLY mechanical given occupancy,
   and the ONLY remaining quantum input is the scale. The sharpest
   possible narrowing; registered at the grade the exclusion proof
   supports.
2. ADMISSIBILITY (the likely outcome, no brick): J0 makes the spinor rep
   natural but does not exclude the singlet; the two bricks are ONE quantum
   PACKAGE (occupancy-plus-selection), not two independent inputs. A real
   sharpening of the wall's structure; hbar and mass untouched.
3. WIDENING (S2 trips): forcing requires a THIRD input beyond {tether,
   J0}; the wall is wider than R's two bricks, and the third input is
   named. An honest negative that corrects the two-brick count.
4. UNDECIDABLE: the {tether, J0} structure does not determine the rep
   selection either way on registered mechanics; registered as such, with
   the specific missing determination named.

## Seal and sequencing
Phase 1 (forcing-vs-admissibility, targets-blind) is one session, self-
sealed by S1-S4. Phase 2 opens ONLY on Phase 1 = forcing. No session runs
both phases. S needs R's frozen tether structure and O's frozen J0 from
the package.

## Stopping rule
One forcing computation, one admissibility-vs-forcing verdict, one
registration. Phase 2 only on forcing. A negative (admissibility,
widening, undecidable) prunes and is kept -- and outcome 2 (the likely one)
is a genuine result: it collapses the two-brick count to a one-package
count and names the package.
