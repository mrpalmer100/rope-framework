# What an Object-Based Ontology of Spacetime Must Satisfy

*A transferable constraint census, distilled from the Rope Hypothesis corpus,
1 August 2026. 400 registered claims, 384 code-backed, 23 registered
Failed-and-kept.*

**Purpose.** This document is deliberately written to be useful **whether or not
the rope model is correct.** It collects the constraints the corpus discovered
while trying to build one specific object-based ontology, stated in a form that
applies to *any* successor that also starts with things rather than with
formalism. Most of them were found the expensive way — by a sector being
retired, an escape closing, or a premise turning out to be load-bearing.

Each entry gives the constraint, where it came from, and — the point of the
document — **what it costs a successor to violate or ignore it.**

---

## I. The constraints that are not negotiable

### 1. Transverse signal speed is exactly c, and this is a budget, not a slogan

*Source: FND-REL-002 (Derived), ELEC-043, ELEC-058.*

In a medium under tension, transverse waves travel at exactly √(T/μ), and if
that speed is c then **no coordination faster than c is available in the
transverse sector.** The consequence is quantitative and it kills things: during
an event of duration τ, only constituents within cτ can participate. In this
corpus that budget closed three separate escapes — the collective-number rescue
(ELEC-043: needed 1e8, causality gives ~1), the bundle escape (ELEC-058), and
persistent circulation (ROPE-SOURCE-AUDIT-002).

**Cost of ignoring it.** Any successor that needs many constituents to act
coherently within a short event must show the coordination is available. Most
proposals silently assume it. Compute cτ first; it is the cheapest possible
falsification of your own idea.

### 2. If the medium has structure, it has a lattice, and lattices violate Lorentz invariance

*Source: FND-REL-003 (EFT-constrained).*

A discrete substrate produces dispersion corrections of order (ka)². Existing
Lorentz-violation bounds then force the spacing **a ≲ 1e-16 m**, which is
sub-nuclear. This is a hard external constraint, it is not going away, and it
costs the ontology something specific: **the substrate decouples from the atomic
scale.** You cannot have a granular spacetime whose grain is anywhere near the
scale of the things you were hoping to explain mechanically.

**Cost of ignoring it.** Any object-based ontology with a length scale inherits
this bound. If your objects are the size of atoms, you are already excluded.

### 3. The bound is a ceiling, and sweeping under it is a real test

*Source: ELEC-057.*

The Lorentz bound is an upper limit, never a measurement, so the substrate scale
is free below it. **Sweep it.** In this corpus that sweep was decisive: the
electron sector's requirement and the ħ sector's requirement passed **5.2
decades apart** with no overlap, and — more damning — the ħ requirement needed a
scale *larger* than the Lorentz bound permits, so it was excluded by the
framework's own two constraints without consulting any experiment.

**Cost of ignoring it.** A successor with two sectors demanding different
substrate scales is dead and can find out cheaply. This is the single
highest-yield test in the document.

### 4. A quantity fixed by an inextensibility constraint cannot be derived locally

*Source: FND-017.*

If the medium is inextensible, its tension is a Lagrange multiplier, not stored
energy. A multiplier is fixed by the state and the boundary conditions — never
by a local property. **Therefore no local derivation of that tension can
succeed**, and any campaign attempting one is misconceived before it starts.

A corollary that surprised this corpus: the ground state stores no energy in
such a tension, so "why doesn't the tensioned vacuum relax?" is not a question.
There is nothing to relax.

### 5. The medium must maintain its tension, or it carries no waves

*Source: ELEC-080.*

Strip away the reservoir — the assumption that an infinite pre-tensioned medium
supplies length to a local disturbance at fixed tension — and a closed medium
**absorbs transverse displacement at exactly zero energy cost**, by shortening
longitudinally wherever it is displaced transversely. Zero cost means no
restoring force, no wave equation, no light.

**Cost of ignoring it.** Any successor whose light is a wave in a medium is
committed to the reservoir premise, whether or not it says so. In this corpus
that premise turned out to underlie the *optics* sector — the strongest results
in the framework — and had been implicit since the first transverse wave was
written down.

## II. The results worth stealing

### 6. Topological quantisation is the cleanest thing an object ontology buys

*Source: EM-001 (Derived).*

Electric charge as a **linking number** makes quantisation forced rather than
imposed: linking numbers are integers because curves cannot pass through one
another, full stop. No successor should give this up cheaply. It is the single
place where "name the thing first" pays a dividend that formalism cannot match —
in a field theory, charge quantisation is an input or a puzzle.

### 7. Composite masses are reachable; fundamental masses are not

*Source: NUC-005/NUC-018 (composite), PM-004 (fundamental, a kept negative).*

Atomic masses from carbon-12 to uranium-238 come out at **0.00–0.51% with one
calibrated constant** — against roughly five fitted parameters in the standard
semi-empirical mass formula. That is a genuine win for structural counting.

The lepton spectrum, by contrast, is registered as an **irreducible input**.
Both halves matter: an object ontology buys you composite structure and does not
by itself buy you the fundamental scale.

### 8. An emergent metric can carry Einstein–Hilbert structure

*Source: GRV-025 (Derived).*

The IR-universal part of the medium's induced action carried the parameter-free
Einstein–Hilbert tensor pattern — responding in the shear channel with the
diagonal and cross channels suppressed. **This is an existence proof**: a
mechanical medium can produce the right tensor structure for gravity without
being told to. Any successor should check whether its own medium does; the test
is a modest lattice computation.

### 9. Invariance theorems collapse parameter counts, and are worth hunting

*Source: ELEC-053, FND-017.*

The single most useful structural result in this corpus was noticing that
T₀/Σ = a²/3 **for any tube radius** — which meant the strand spacing never
depended on quantities the programme had spent a session adjudicating, and later
that the tension was not independent either. The framework went from several
apparently free scales to **one**.

**Advice.** Before measuring anything, look for ratios that are invariant under
the deformations you are worried about. They are cheap to find and they retire
whole disputes.

## III. The failure modes, which cost the most to learn

### 10. Ask what *sources* a quantity before building the apparatus to measure it

*Source: `STANDING_RULE_SOURCE_BEFORE_INSTRUMENT.md`; two closed branches.*

This corpus built a validated 3-D lattice-gauge solver across five sessions,
then discovered in one session of reading that **nothing in the framework
sources a flux** — and worse, that the one topological circulation it does
derive (2πN) lands exactly in the instrument's null space, being spectrally
invisible in a 2π-periodic spectrum.

Separately, a pilot-wave programme of seven claims turned out never to have
depended on the physical scale it was said to inherit.

**The rule.** Before instrument work: name the claim that sources the quantity,
state the magnitude it predicts, and check the predicted value is not in the
instrument's null space. The audit costs one session. The alternative cost
roughly eight.

### 11. Distinguish *distinctive* from *derived*, or your prediction list is fiction

*Source: ELEC-062 → ELEC-063 → ELEC-064 → GRV-049.*

This corpus believed it had eight distinctive predictions. A census against four
criteria — quantitative, **distinctive in observable outcome**, checkable, live —
reduced that to **one**. The demotions came in recognisable kinds, and a
successor will meet all of them:

- **Confirmed but shared.** Cosmic birefringence flatness is confirmed by Planck
  data — and the standard axion predicts it too.
- **Conditional on an unestablished population.** No-evaporation predictions need
  primordial black holes nobody has found.
- **Awaiting an unbuilt model.** A discriminating branch that requires a
  component the framework has not built is a specification, not a prediction.
- **Falsifiable but not confirmable.** A value sitting at the floor every rival
  also predicts can kill you and cannot vindicate you.
- **Derivation-distinctive only.** Deriving a number others *fit* is a real
  achievement and does not discriminate.

**Deriving what others fit is worth doing. It is not evidence against them.**

### 12. A truncation can be right about scaling and wrong about shape

*Source: ELEC-073 → ELEC-074.*

A sixth-order expansion gave a soliton profile cusped at the origin. Solved to
all orders, the true object is **hollow with a hard core at finite radius** — a
qualitatively different thing. Yet the *scaling relations* (size linear in the
field excursion, energy cubic) survived exactly, with only coefficients moving.

**The lesson.** Exponents come from dimensional structure a truncation cannot
damage; profiles come from dynamics it can. Trust truncated exponents; distrust
truncated shapes.

### 13. Derrick's theorem is dimension-dependent, so 1+1 results are not results

*Source: ELEC-070.*

A stabilisation mechanism that worked beautifully in one dimension — internal
circulation balancing collapse, giving the pleasing L\* = c/ω — **does not exist
in three**, where every term of dE/dL is positive and there is no stationary
point at all. Any soliton claim in 1+1 must be redone in 3+1 before it means
anything.

### 14. Check whether a *later* claim revised the one you are using

*Source: GRV-053, and HBAR-010's inherited premise.*

Twice in one day this corpus built on a superseded result. In the worse case a
luminosity law had been revised three claims later in the same sector, under a
title announcing the revision in capitals — and the resulting number was wrong
by **63 orders of magnitude**. The conclusion survived, which is not a defence:
right for the wrong reason is what this discipline exists to prevent.

**Both failures were caught by a human reading, not by any automated check.** A
dependency sweep that looks *downstream* of changed claims will not catch them.
You need a *forward* check: given a claim about to be used, list every later
claim in its sector.

### 15. Keep your failures, and let them count against you

*Source: 23 registered Failed-and-kept claims; THM-006.*

The most useful single feature of this corpus is that failures stay in the
registry with their reasoning. It made today's retirements possible — you cannot
retire a sector honestly if the record has been groomed.

The sharpest instance: a theorem about where failures cluster **fell below its
own pre-registered bar** as the corpus grew, and was reclassified Failed rather
than having the bar relaxed. It then turned out to be classified in the very
category it was theorising about, so its own failure counted as evidence *for*
itself — a self-reference trap that had to be excluded explicitly.

## IV. What a successor would inherit

An object-based ontology of spacetime attempting to replace this one starts
with:

- a substrate scale **below 1e-16 m**, forced;
- a transverse coordination budget of **exactly c**, and three worked examples
  of what it kills;
- **no local derivation of a constraint tension**, established;
- a requirement to maintain tension or forfeit wave propagation;
- topological charge quantisation as a template that works;
- composite masses reachable, fundamental masses not;
- an existence proof that a mechanical medium can carry Einstein–Hilbert
  structure;
- and a documented list of the ways a programme like this fools itself.

**What it does not inherit is the rope.** Nothing above requires strands
specifically. The constraints came from *having objects with a scale, a tension,
and a signal speed* — which is what any successor in this family will also have.
